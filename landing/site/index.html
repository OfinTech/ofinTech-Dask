Every figure traced to its source.

OfinTech Desk is an AI workspace for finance teams. It reads your general ledger, spreadsheets, documents and inbox, does the arithmetic in code, and gives CFOs, accountants and managers numbers they can open up to the ledger lines behind them. Nothing is sent or changed until a person approves it.

Features

Financial analysis

35 KPIs on your targets, not a benchmark

Growth and profitability, liquidity, working capital, leverage, cash flow, and cost and control, computed in code from the general ledger. The finance lead sets a target and a warning level for each one, and the card shows on target, watch or act.

  • Any chart of accounts. Each account gets a role such as cash, debt, payroll or depreciation, suggested from its name and confirmed by a person.
  • Formulas you can read and change. DSO is receivables / SUMN(revenue, 3) * 91.25. Edit any formula or add KPIs of your own.
  • Open any value to see its 24-month history, the measures in the formula and the ledger accounts behind them.
  • Real balances. Month-end balances are rebuilt from the synced balance sheet; cash flow is split into operating, investing and financing.

Growth and profitability

Revenue growth, gross, EBITDA, operating and net margins, return on equity, assets and capital employed.

Liquidity

Current, quick and cash ratios, working capital, days of cash on hand.

Working capital

DSO, DPO, DIO, cash conversion cycle, overdue receivables and payables, collection rate.

Leverage

Debt to equity, net debt to EBITDA, interest cover, debt service coverage, equity ratio.

Cash flow

Operating and free cash flow, cash runway, cash conversion of profit.

Cost and control

Opex and payroll to revenue, margin of safety, revenue against plan, forecast accuracy, customer concentration.

Forecasting

Your assumptions, our arithmetic, the CFO's approval

Choose a method for every P&L line: growth, same month last year, a percentage of another line, an average, a trend, a fixed amount or month by month. Add receivable, inventory and payable days, capital spending and debt repayment. The forecast projects profit, working capital and cash, and names the month cash would run out.

  • Scenarios and versions, compared side by side.
  • Draft → submitted → approved. Only the finance lead approves or makes a forecast the plan, and approved figures are frozen.
  • The plan feeds the revenue against plan and forecast accuracy KPIs, and each approved forecast shows its error and bias as months close.
  • A statistical forecast (Holt or Holt-Winters) is drawn next to yours, with its measured error, as a check. It never replaces your assumptions.

Cost centres

Follow a project through the ledger, the journal and the bank

  • Rules instead of a ledger field. Combine tags, accounts, suppliers, words and dates, so it works on books that were never tagged. The untagged share shows how much a rule picked up rather than a tag.
  • Cost against cash. What was paid directly, which supplier bills are paid or still unpaid, and which bank payments may belong to the project.
  • The date the budget runs out at the current pace, overall and for each budget line.
  • Budgets from your own file. Read from the Excel or Word file the team already uses; every line keeps the cell it came from.

Templates to fill in, and a ledger upload

Two Excel templates download from the Cost centres page, with a guide sheet and drop-down lists in each:

  • General ledger template: one row per journal line, with date, entry, type, account code and name, account class, debit, credit, description, party and cost-centre tags. Fill it in, or export your ledger with the same columns.
  • Budget template: code, item, amount and notes, with a total line. Read it into a cost centre and each line is matched to ledger accounts by code or name.

Upload the ledger as Excel or CSV. The upload reports the lines, entries and dates it found and names any entry whose debits and credits differ. Next month, upload the new export to the same ledger and every cost centre built on it is brought up to date.

Connections and webhooks

Connected to the systems around the ledger

QuickBooks Online, Xero, Zoho Books and Odoo sync read-only. Gmail, Drive and Sheets, Telegram and WhatsApp, PipesHub for scanned documents, and any Model Context Protocol server give the assistant more to work with, each tool asking for approval unless you allow it to run on its own.

Webhooks, under Connections, link anything with HTTPS:

  • Incoming: an address for your ERP, bank portal, ticketing or CI system to call, with a secret it presents as a bearer token or an HMAC-SHA256 signature. A delivery can start a workflow with the JSON it carried; the workflow's own approval steps still apply.
  • Outgoing: signed JSON posted to your address when things happen here: approvals decided, forecasts approved, files revised, cost centres changed, workflow runs finished. Choose the event groups; failed deliveries are retried after 1, 10 and 60 minutes.
  • Secrets are shown once and stored encrypted; every delivery is logged with its response and can be sent again.

The workspace

Built for work that someone will audit

The assistant is useful only if you can check it. Every feature is designed so a controller can see where a number came from and who approved what.

Evidence-linked workbooks and reports

Inputs drill down to the rows they came from, totals are live Excel formulas, and figures without a source are shaded. A separate reviewer model checks each file without seeing the conversation.

Your own Excel and Word files

Attach a company template or last month's pack. The assistant fills it in, keeps your layout and formulas, saves a new version and highlights any figure it cannot trace.

Approvals before anything leaves

E-mails, chat messages, workflow runs and external tools wait for an editor to approve the exact content. Every decision is in the audit log.

Memory you approve

The assistant proposes lasting facts such as a materiality threshold or an account mapping; nothing is kept until a person saves it. Each answer shows which memories it used.

One search with citations

Documents (scans read with OCR), past conversations, memory, files and data in one place, with a short answer that cites the page it came from.

Workflows for the close

Month-end packs, receivables follow-up and bills due, on a schedule, from an e-mail or from a webhook, with retries and a timeline of every run.

Any model, fewer tokens

OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini, DeepSeek, Mistral, Azure or a local model through Ollama. Requests carry only the tools they need and are ordered for prompt caching.

Four languages, right to left

English, Arabic, Spanish and French, chosen per person. Arabic is set in IBM Plex Sans Arabic and laid out right to left; the assistant answers in the language you write in.

Install

Run it on your own server or computer

Most teams run the Team Server and give everyone the desktop app. Your data stays on your infrastructure; the only outside calls go to the model provider and the systems you connect.

For a team

Team Server

Docker Compose with automatic HTTPS. Accounts, invitations, roles, approvals inbox, webhooks, sign-in from your website.

For each person

Desktop app

macOS and Windows client of your Team Server, or a standalone app with its own engine on one computer.

For contributors

From source

Python 3.11+ and Node.js 22+. Engine, UI and tests in one repository.

Team Server on Ubuntu 24.04

You need a server with 2 vCPU and 4 GB of RAM (up to 25 people), a DNS A record for your domain pointing at it, and ports 80 and 443 open.

  1. 01

    Install Docker and Compose

    sudo apt update && sudo apt install -y ca-certificates curl
sudo install -m 0755 -d /etc/apt/keyrings
sudo curl -fsSL https://download.docker.com/linux/ubuntu/gpg -o /etc/apt/keyrings/docker.asc
echo "deb [arch=$(dpkg --print-architecture) signed-by=/etc/apt/keyrings/docker.asc] \
  https://download.docker.com/linux/ubuntu $(. /etc/os-release && echo $VERSION_CODENAME) stable" \
  | sudo tee /etc/apt/sources.list.d/docker.list
sudo apt update && sudo apt install -y docker-ce docker-ce-cli containerd.io docker-compose-plugin
  2. 02

    Get the code and create the settings file

    git clone https://github.com/OfinTech/ofinTech-Dask.git
cd ofinTech-Dask/server
cp .env.example .env && chmod 600 .env
  3. 03

    Generate the master key

    It encrypts every stored credential and file. Keep a copy in a password manager, separate from your backups.

    KEY=$(python3 -c 'import secrets,base64;print(base64.urlsafe_b64encode(secrets.token_bytes(32)).decode())')
sed -i "s|^DESK_MASTER_KEY=.*|DESK_MASTER_KEY=$KEY|" .env
  4. 04

    Set your domain, website, e-mail and sign-in

    # .env
DESK_DOMAIN=desk.yourcompany.com
ACME_EMAIL=it@yourcompany.com
DESK_OPEN_SIGNUP=false                # invitation only
DESK_SITE_URL=https://yourcompany.com # optional: a website whose forms sign people in

# optional: invitations and password resets by e-mail
DESK_SMTP_HOST=smtp.yourprovider.com
DESK_SMTP_USER=desk@yourcompany.com
DESK_SMTP_PASSWORD=...
DESK_SMTP_FROM=OfinTech Desk <desk@yourcompany.com>

# optional: "Continue with Google", limited to your domain
DESK_GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID=...
DESK_GOOGLE_CLIENT_SECRET=...
DESK_GOOGLE_ALLOWED_DOMAINS=yourcompany.com
  5. 05

    Start it and create the administrator

    docker compose up -d --build
docker compose logs -f engine      # wait for: Uvicorn running on http://0.0.0.0:8700

    Open your domain. The first account becomes the administrator, so create it straight away. Then add a model provider under Settings → Models and invite colleagues under Settings → Members.

  6. 06

    Back up every night

    ./backup.sh                        # backups/<timestamp>.sqlite3 with a checksum
# crontab -e
0 2 * * * cd /path/to/ofinTech-Dask/server && ./backup.sh

    Optional: keep files in MinIO or any S3 store with -f docker-compose.minio.yml. Full checklist in docs/DEPLOYMENT.md.

Connect a ledger

Works with any accounting system

Four systems connect out of the box. Anything else, from Tally and Sage to SAP Business One or your own database, links through the Open Accounting Connector, a small documented API, or arrives as a general ledger file through the template above. Every connection is read-only.

Each connection syncs profit and loss by month, the balance sheet, invoices and bills into datasets, with ready-made dashboards. Set it up under Connections → Accounting (admin role).

Pull

OfinTech Desk reads your adapter

For a live system with an API or a database you can query. You run a small HTTPS service; OfinTech Desk calls it on every sync and when the assistant reads a report.

GET /v1/company
GET /v1/profit-and-loss?start=2026-01-01&end=2026-03-31
GET /v1/balance-sheet?as_of=2026-03-31
GET /v1/invoices?status=open|all
GET /v1/bills?status=open|all

Authorization: Bearer <adapter key>
  1. Copy examples/accounting-adapter/adapter.py (standard library only).
  2. Replace its four data functions with queries against your system, using a read-only user.
  3. Serve it over HTTPS, then add Other accounting system → Pull with its address and key.

Push

Your system sends JSON

For systems behind a firewall or without an API: a nightly export posts to the Team Server. Nothing is opened towards your network.

curl -X POST \
  https://desk.yourcompany.com/api/ingest/v1/accounting/profit-and-loss \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer odk_…" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"lines":[{"period":"2026-03-01","section":"Income",
       "account":"4000 Sales","amount":53000}]}'
# → {"kind":"profit-and-loss","rows":2,"dataset_id":"ds_…"}
  1. Add Other accounting system → Push and copy the ingest key; it is shown once.
  2. Post company, profit-and-loss, balance-sheet, invoices and bills.
  3. Each post replaces that data set; send the full history you want to analyse.

The full specification is an OpenAPI 3.1 file, ready for Swagger UI or Postman: docs/openapi/accounting-connector.json. Guides: docs/ACCOUNTING_API.md, docs/WEBHOOKS.md, docs/COST_CENTRES.md.

Plainly stated

What it does not claim

  • KPIs and forecasts are management figures computed from your ledger. They are not audited statements and not financial advice; there are no industry benchmarks, only your own targets and history.
  • Tax, accounting and regulatory output is general guidance. No certification or compliance is claimed for e-invoicing, data protection laws or any regulator.
  • Accounting connectors read data; they do not post entries. Cash comes from the ledger's bank lines; there is no live bank connection.
  • The independent reviewer is a second model check, not an audit. Test every connection against a sandbox company before using it with real books.
